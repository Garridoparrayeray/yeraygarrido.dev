Abstract

Journey planners for public transport usually compute routes on a server. This paper describes and evaluates one that computes them entirely on the passenger’s device, in the browser, over fifteen merged transit networks in and around the Basque Country (8,242 stops, 54,896 trips). Offline it answers from the last timetables downloaded; online it repairs each answer with the real-time state of the trips shown. Its search is a multi-criteria range variant of round-based public transit routing on arrival and walking time, with bags of four labels that never discard the earliest arrival and lower bounds on the remaining time and rides computed once per target; a selection layer turns the result into five labelled options. On the production data, four labels find 88% of the exact restricted Pareto set without changing the first option of 2,000 plan queries, and the bounds make plan queries 2.6 times faster. Earliest arrivals agree with an independent Connection Scan search and with an exhaustive enumeration of small networks. Against OpenTripPlanner on identical data and without pruning of walking options, each planner returns almost every journey of the other that its own walking rules allow, to within 60 s (ours) and 120 s (OpenTripPlanner); with the production walking table, ours returns 96% of those of OpenTripPlanner within 60 s. Delaying or cancelling a third of the trips adds half to the query time. In single runs, the median plan query took 16 ms on a recent iPhone and 65 ms on a 2021 mid-range Android phone.
