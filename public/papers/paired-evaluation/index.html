Abstract

Real-time bus arrival information is a critical component of public transit usability, yet many regional transit operators either rely on proprietary prediction engines of opaque accuracy or publish raw vehicle-position feeds without explicit schedule-deviation fields.

This study presents a large-scale paired empirical evaluation comparing the open-data Bide+ arrival prediction method against the official operator application on the Bizkaibus interurban bus network in Biscay, Spain. Evaluating 555,744 strictly paired predictions against actual GPS ground-truth passages across 59 stops, the Bide+ method achieves 64% accuracy within a ±2-minute tolerance window compared to 39% for the official operator application (+25 percentage points), outperforming the official predictor at 57 out of 59 evaluated stops.
